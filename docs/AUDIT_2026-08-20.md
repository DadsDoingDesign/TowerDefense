# Fieldwatch — External GameDev Audit (2026-08-20)

Branch `GameDev_Audit` @ 318a81d. Eight audit passes (engine, data/systems, run/meta,
UI/UX, feel/render, balance harness, live Playwright walkthrough, build checks) plus an
adversarial verification loop (11/14 high-severity claims confirmed — 4 proven live —
2 corrected, 0 refuted) and a completeness critic pass.

**80 findings: 6 critical, 24 high, 38 medium, 12 low — every one mapped to a
workstream below (80/80 coverage).** Full styled report with the complete register:
the "Fieldwatch Audit" artifact (claude.ai/code/artifact/782b5a4d-b23e-424b-8c04-3cb4e00d9d89).

## The five structural problems

1. **The sim can't be trusted.** No run seed (`gameStore.ts:91,684`), variable
   timestep (`BattleCanvas.tsx:57,64` — 3× is a different battle), DoT/trap kills
   award no XP (`engine.ts:419,572`).
2. **The shipping shell hides/misstates the game.** Dark Sacrifice unreachable;
   endless recruit is a fake choice (always hires `recruitOptions[0]`); hero-pick
   tiles fabricate mechanics ("+4 dodge" doesn't exist); "Erase everything" wipes the
   save in one unconfirmed tap; forge has no costs and silently no-ops; stacking
   rules (`mergeMods` strongest-wins) surfaced nowhere.
3. **Nothing survives a phone.** No mid-run persistence (tab eviction = run lost,
   zero marks), no PWA, no safe-area, no error boundary, iOS audio-unlock holes.
4. **Difficulty/meta is a treadmill.** Meta is 100% vertical creep; MC deaths cluster
   at the two elite nodes and the boss kills nobody; fresh-player band never
   simulated; winning is a dead end; endless bosses are 10× weaker than the normal
   wave before them; L20 evolutions arrive at the final two nodes.
5. **Watching isn't fun.** No particles/hitstop/shake/music; `'hit'` sfx is dead code;
   chain lightning draws nothing; wave-clear is a same-frame state transition; static
   terrain re-renders every frame (~90% of frame cost).

## Phase 2 — COMPLETE (implement → 3-reviewer adversarial pass → fix)

Delivered WS4 (build honesty), WS6 (difficulty/meta/endgame), WS9 (onboarding/a11y),
WS5-rest (shell parity), WS11 (hygiene), plus M9 loot shaping. `npm run balance` went
from **20 failing invariants to all-passing**, and the review then found more.

The phase's defining lesson: **three "design failures" were measurement failures, and
three CRITICAL defects passed a fully green scoreboard.** Green invariants are a floor,
never a ceiling.

Measurement failures corrected (each would have sent a rebalance the wrong way):
- The support sweep demanded surviving 612 goblins at ×30 HP in 8.4s — ~12x anything
  the game generates. Proven by testing the control: an empty slot held ×12.26, an
  empty slot plus a real damage tower ×12.59. A bar a damage tower cannot clear is not
  a damage-tower bar.
- The 0% fresh-player rate came from a model that forced 10 straight battles and
  refused the merchants and recruits the game guarantees.
- The enchant bench scored a 47-second fight while calling itself a ~100-second hold.
- §1's outlier gate could not fail (spawn-bound wave compressed a 14x analytic spread
  into 1.8x); rebuilt as saturated throughput, spread now 16.3x.
- §11 graded ONE hardcoded routing policy — and it was the worst line available
  (28% vs 41% for the best). It also measured a pre-M9 loot table the game had stopped
  shipping. Fixing both moved the same line +13pt.
- Every UI harness hard-coded 390x844, and click success was being read as
  reachability — Playwright scrolls `overflow:hidden` boxes that a finger cannot.

CRITICAL defects the review caught behind green gates:
- **Body armour silently disabled pierce.** `engine.impact` made splash and pierce
  mutually exclusive, and every body item rolls `splashAdd` from common upward — so
  pierce was dead for essentially every hero in every run, killing the Marksman line,
  the `piercing` enchant and Piercing Volley (which Phase 2 had just re-costed to
  −70% damage for a mechanic that never fired). Fixed: a Ranger wearing an epic body
  went from a 12% to a 100% stop rate on a depth-9 wave.
- **The game was unplayable on an iPhone SE.** The four-band shell needed 737px; at
  375x667 `Start Wave` sat below the viewport with `elementFromPoint` returning null
  and no way to scroll. The device matrix then exposed three further defects nobody
  had measured: a 240px canvas floor clipping the build slots off a short Stage, hero
  tabs at 27-43px on every phone under 390 wide, and a pack header running under its
  own sort button.
- **The printed stacking rule was false.** "You keep the best of each number" — but
  `mergeMods` multiplies mults, sums adds, and takes-best only for statuses. Close
  Quarters + Whetstone Pact promised +22% damage and delivered +9.8%. Auditing its
  neighbours found the mirror bug: `physDefAdd` was merged, applied and mitigated real
  damage while being described nowhere.
- **Cartographer's Table was a trap purchase** — a 120-mark "horizontal unlock" that
  took the campaign from ~20% winnable to ~2%, permanently. Now it buys forks instead
  of length (choiceless steps 49%→11%, mixed forks 25%→49%) and pays for them with two
  stops. **Chronicler was deleted**: 880 of ~1,990 hub marks, the correct first click
  on any horizon, and it changed nothing about a run.
- **The Banner ladder's economy was inverted** — rung 1 free money, rungs 3-5 strictly
  ignorable (marks flatlined while win rate collapsed 19%→0%). Rebuilt as 3 monotonic
  rungs. "Forced March" was cut on evidence: removing merchants is a *buff*, because a
  stop is worth negative to a fresh run.

UI truthfulness failures shipped and fixed this phase: FOUR (old Dark Sacrifice copy,
"walking away costs nothing", Banner 2's inherited restriction, Banner 5 restating
rung 3). Every one was caught by checking strings against the engine rather than
reading them for plausibility.

New invariants added so these classes cannot ship green again: no hub purchase may
lower the measured win rate; Banner rungs must cost win rate AND raise expected marks
monotonically; curses need two-sided cost; affix benches are pinned with a baseline
range; §11 gates the floor on the best policy and the ceiling on the first-timer line.

Open, deliberately deferred to Phase 3: one battlefield and fully deterministic wave
composition (WS8 — the tactical layer still has zero input randomness); mystic offense
specs measure 0.13-0.32x the median throughput (reported, not gated); a stop is worth
negative to a fresh run (stop economy); the campaign opening kit is dealt before the
hero is picked.

## Phase 3 — COMPLETE (implement ×2 waves → 2-reviewer adversarial pass → fix → close-out)

Delivered WS7 (feel/audio/spectacle), WS8 (run variety), and the art-pipeline and
icon work from the companion art audit. `npm run balance` returned to ALL-PASS.

**The finding that reframes the whole project.** A pre-existing engine bug —
`updateEnemies`/`updateTraps` iterating `this.enemies` while `killEnemy` spliced
that same array — meant **every damage-over-time kill silently deleted the enemy
standing behind it**: no gold, no XP, no kill credit, no leak damage. Measured
across 720 real waves: 17.2% of waves lost a body, 0.83% of all spawns vanished.

It had inflated every DoT mechanic's measured value for the life of the project.
With it fixed, `Incendiary` went from **+16.7pt to −6.3pt** — a mutation whose
entire apparent value was the bug, and whose stated downside was unpaid-for.
`flaming` 32.3→9.9, `frost` 56.2→49.1, `Siphon` +35.6→+7.4. Every balance
judgement this project made about burn was made against a broken engine. The
re-cost that followed is the first honest measurement DoT has ever had here.

Defects the review caught behind a fully green scoreboard (4 critical, 13 major):
- **Every burning enemy rendered as a featureless white blob** for the whole burn
  — the tick differ read continuous DoT as a discrete hit, so the flash never
  decayed (42 of 44 frames pinned). One of four shipped procs erased every
  readability channel Phases 2 and 3 paid for.
- **The drawn knockback never happened** — 0 of 988 samples; step 5 overwrote it.
- **The palisade gate lied about base HP** — 4 states not 7, drew intact at 85%,
  and *inverted* to full at the moment of defeat, on the one channel written to
  survive colour-vision differences.
- **The icon atlas shipped quantised to 16 colours** (25 authored), so the
  phys/magic damage mark — "the single property that decides whether a drop is
  worth anything" — shipped as tan vs grey.
- **The wave strip covered up to 40% of the battlefield**, including build slots,
  on four of five viewports. Nobody saw it because 390×844 is the one viewport
  whose geometry hides it, and every render was taken there.
- 1,935 non-axis-aligned rotations per frame that the 1.000 sprite-scale metric
  reported as clean, because it checked size ratios and not transforms.
- `itemIcon` drawing the wrong object for 25 keepsake names; `effectIcon`
  stamping speed-lines on the three slowest items in the game.

Why they all passed green: **the gates measure what someone thought to measure.**
`fxStats` counted particles and hitstops but never looked at `flash`. §14b measured
composition distance on the registry key, so elite variants were disjoint by
construction and always scored 100%. Each is now instrumented for the thing that
was missing.

Also this phase: an original procedural score in code (no third-party audio, no
licence risk, +3.7 KB gzipped); a second battlefield with seeded wave and elite
variety, ending the "placement is solved once" finding; and boot payload cut from
210 requests / 987 KB to **136 files / 1,396 KB precached**, with 132 unreferenced
files excluded.

### Phase 3, round 3 — the round where convergence broke

The fix layer came back at 2 critical + 6 major, *worse* than the round before it,
and the reason is the most transferable lesson in the project.

**The critical defect was an interaction between two fixes made in the same round.**
One agent tightened the DoT/impact threshold; another raised Incendiary's burn from
45 to 180. Each verified its own work. Together the raised burn multiplied the other's
threshold 1.25 → 3.5, and because that predicate subtracted an *un-resisted* burn
while the engine resists it, an armoured column over-charged attrition by 1.65/tick —
**3.3× the entire floor**. Result: ~10% of all hits swallowed, drawn as attrition,
the shot vanishing while a damage number still floated. Parallel agents with clean
file ownership do not collide on files — **they collide on shared constants**, and
nothing in the process was watching for that.

The fix removed the bound entirely and re-runs the engine's own arithmetic per damage
type, making it magnitude-independent: min margin now **1.998 at burn 45/90/180/360/900**,
identical to three decimals. It also revealed the old floor was never a bound on
thorns (0.800/tick) or traps (0.500/tick) either.

Other findings that round: **Warden of Ash**, an irreversible level-20 pick, was sold
on "everything it holds burns on its thorns" — thorns route through `damageEnemy`,
which writes no statuses, so they had never ignited anything (fixed at the mechanic
via a declared `thornsIgnite` capability, after measuring that a universal
every-blocker rule would delete Incendiary's only cost). Elite *and* normal wave
variants were compositionally identical at depth 1 (0.0% apart) because §14b only
ever gated depths 4/7/9 — **the silent skip is what let it survive**. §8 had no
ceiling, so no mutation could fail for being too strong. Reward cards were graded by
nothing at all, and their rarity ladder was inverted (+0.8/+3.4/+3.7/+0.5 — two of
three legendaries net-negative, the best card in the game a rare).

Two clamps — `frost` and `executioner` — had been paying the Epic tier a Legendary
value, and the campaign curve was fitted with that inflation baked in: removing both
dropped §6 from 48% to **42%**. Compensated at the rarity budget where the inflation
lived, and flagged as the global gear buff it is.

Closing state: `npm run balance` all-pass across §1–§15; regression battery 232
assertions, 0 failures; device matrix and a11y 0 failures across 10 cells; cold first
run 7 taps, 0 dead taps.

Known and deliberately unresolved, with measurements in the code: the environment's
tier-4 gold residue (structural — brown *is* 30–60°); traps remain one static 34px
charge on a 2290px lane (the **copy** was corrected, not the mechanic — carpeting the
path is a placement system, out of scope for a close-out); `§5`'s pressure ceiling now
sits at ×2.20 against a ×2–8 band, the thinnest headroom in the suite; and key art for
the main menu is still unbought.
